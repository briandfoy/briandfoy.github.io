Okay, not everything is electrons, but I want to discuss how almost everything we know is electrons. Or, more correctly, the electromagnetic force which we interact with through electrons. Or, closer to the point I want to make, we don't use the other fundamental forces that much.

<!--more-->

We convert almost everything to electricity and record that. Perhaps the step before that was light, which is the vector of the electromagnetic force. Gravity waves? We measure precise and accurate distances with lasers. And so on and so on.

All of that is EM engineering: harnessing the electromagnetic to get only what we want and exclude what we don't. Although the FAA tells you to turn off your cell phones on planes, does anyone really do that beyond saving battery. Heck, at least Delta lets you use WiFi while you are still on the ground, where you previously had to wait to get to 10,000 feet. Your computer speakers probably don't buzz and click right before you receive a call. Microwaves ovens are better shielded so we don't mistake them for aliens.

In experiments, you want shielded cables that don't influence the current in nearby cables. Indeed, I once had to compute the magnetic field around a particular coaxial cable to check if we could ignore its effect, especially if it moved relative to the earth's magnetic field. Well, I had to do that because the supervising scientist was a dick, but whatever.

Along with that, where it matters, there is clean power. In hospital rooms, look for the different colored outlets. One is regular, crappy power, and one is medical grade for the fancy machines.

Similarly, audio engineers spend a lot of time reducing sources of noise with clean power, high-end cables, good connectors, and many other things before the electronics do their digital magic.
