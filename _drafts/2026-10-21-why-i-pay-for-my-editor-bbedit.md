I started using BBEdit some time in the late 1990s, when I was looking for something different from Metrowerk's CodeWarrior, which was itself much better than the editor Apple was shipping with MPW. Wow, that was 30 years ago, and there's one good reason I kept paying for a closed-source text editor besides it being an excellent editor for my needs.

<!--more-->

I sent Bare Bones, the makers of <a href="https://www.barebones.com/products/bbedit/index.html>BBEdit</a>, this support message at 1 AM, with a couple screenshots and an example file that demonstrated the problem:

<blockquote>
BBEdit doesn't add some Perl subroutine definitions mixing attributes and signatures to the function list. These likely affect no one but me, and it's not a huge issue for me. BBEdit has probably had this behavior for years, and I only noticed in BBEdit 15 when I started working on code that included the rarely used attributes features. This isn't blocking or disrupting anything I'm doing, and I can simply search for `sub some_name` to get where I want.
</blockquote>

Rich Siegel got back to me 10 hours later to say it's fixed.

<blockquote>
I've been able to duplicate the symptom you describe, and a fix is in place for a future update. (Not the very next due to timing, but one subsequent.)
</blockquote>

Think about that timing. I sent the message in the middle of the night, and it's fixed before lunch (although in the next release). It's an esoteric bug, but someone investigated it, fixed it, and schedule it for release before I woke.

Not only that, in the few bugs I have reported over the years, I've sometimes been given a new build to try that same week, which means I have the fix before it's in a general release.

That alone is worth the $30 I pay for an upgrade every year or so. It's definitely worth $30 to not have some open source GNU nut lecture me about my political choices by participating in commerce, or some maintainer saying "pull request or gtfo", or even worse, being completely warnocked.
